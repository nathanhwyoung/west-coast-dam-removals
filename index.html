About this map

Every dam removal recorded in California, Oregon and Washington, drawn against the river habitat the fish actually use.

The dam records come from the American Rivers Dam Removal Database, the national register of removals, in its March 2026 release. This map covers the three West Coast states: 333 rows, 332 distinct removals, 290 of them placed on the map. The rest have no coordinate that any source can supply, and the section below accounts for all of them.

Underneath the dams are two kinds of fish layer for 27 species: federally designated ESA critical habitat, and where each species is mapped as present by the state and federal agencies that survey them. Both are drawn at display resolution, for looking at. The analysis behind the project asked a separate question of the full-resolution data, and is described under Not yet on this map.

This is an independent project and not affiliated with any of the agencies whose data it uses. Where it has changed or questioned a source record, it says so.

What the map draws

One switch per species; the arrow beside it opens separate ESA and presence switches, to see either on its own. Thin dark line: ESA critical habitat. Wide soft band underneath: where the fish are mapped (presence). The two weights mean how consistent the source is, not how certain it is. ESA critical habitat is one federal designation defined the same way in all three states, so it gets one precise line. Presence is assembled from ODFW, WDFW, CDFW, NOAA, USFWS and PISCES, at different dates and different grains, so it is drawn as a soft envelope rather than given a crispness it has not got. Colours are placeholders, ordered so species that share map area never get neighbouring colours. Two species mapped on the same reach cannot both be drawn there: whichever comes later in the list covers the one before it. The colour ordering makes that covering visible rather than invisible, and the per-species switches are how to see either on its own.

California ranges

In California, 11 species' presence is not a stream at all: it is whole HUC12 watersheds from PISCES, 1,597 of them, median width about 3 km. Drawn as outlines they would read as a web of rivers, and where one is cut at the state line its edge draws along the border as if fish lived on it. So each species' watersheds are merged into their outer boundary and drawn as a faint fill, which removes the internal seams: 1,597 rings become 357. It does far more for some species than others (Chinook 766 → 41, but green sturgeon 196 → 114, whose watersheds are scattered rather than touching). Display only. The join reads the full-resolution watersheds one at a time.

Comparing Oregon with California for one species compares two mapping models, not two abundances. Oregon and Washington map presence as stream reaches; California maps it as watersheds. A species can look far more widespread in California purely because the unit is larger.

Display geometry

Simplified at 0.0003° and rounded to about 11 m. Fine for looking at, never used for a join or a measurement: those read the full-resolution source. Habitat lying wholly in Idaho, Montana or Nevada is left off, since no dam here is near it; anything touching or within 3 km of California, Oregon or Washington stays.

Shaded relief sharpens as you zoom in, swapping a 500 m elevation model for a 115 m one. Relief and lakes are both clipped to California, Oregon and Washington, so nothing is shaded outside the three states this project covers.

Why this looks different

Redrawn in D3 with an Albers equal-area conic projection, replacing a MapLibre map on CARTO raster tiles. CARTO began watermarking its free tiles with "API KEY REQUIRED", and the previous basemap is unusable without an account.

The change is also a small methods improvement. A web tile map is locked to Web Mercator, which inflates area with latitude. Across this data (33.6°N to 48.9°N) that stretches the north end by 1.27× relative to the south, so Washington read about 27% larger than it should against southern California. An equal-area conic is the honest choice over a north-south extent, and this project measures in projected coordinate systems everywhere else.

Nothing here is fetched from another server. No basemap, no tile service, no API key, no analytics, no fonts, no CDN. Every file the page loads is served alongside it, including the one library it uses, D3 v7.9.0, so the map works offline and leaves no trace with a third party. State outlines are US Census cartographic boundaries, public domain, 39 KB.

Not yet on this map

Both joins have run and been audited. Every dam was asked which ESA critical habitat and which of 21 native migratory fish lie within 75 m, with a same-stream and a watershed-range clause. The answers are in each popup. They are not drawn: the habitat and species presence above are shown at display resolution. Proximity on screen is still not a result.

Also absent: the line from a dam to the reaches it matched, upstream traces, and any mileage figure.

333 rows, 290 mapped. American Rivers left 52 rows without a coordinate. 12 were recovered by linking to the NACC barrier inventory. The other 40 cannot be placed from any source; 18 of them are California dams removed between 1925 and 1951, several on forks of the Trinity, Salmon and Scott. The coordinate hunt is closed: historical topographic maps are the only instrument left.

Two coordinates are voided. CA-157 and CA-158, the Silverado Creek fish dams, carry coordinates copied from two San Juan Creek dams 24.6 km away in a different basin. They are still counted as removals; they cannot be placed, so they are not drawn and will not be joined to anything.

One row is a duplicate. CA-091 repeats CA-148: both are TCFD11 on Trabuco Creek, same height, same barrier ID, 12 m apart. Unlike a voided coordinate, a duplicate is not a removal, so it leaves the count as well as the map. 333 rows, 332 removals. Both numbers are shown because they are different claims.

Structures against sites. 22 rows fall in 10 clusters that NACC records as a single barrier each, so 332 removals sit at 320 places. Nothing is hidden: the map draws every structure. But a distance join cannot tell members of a cluster apart, so identical species results across one are geometry, not evidence.
