This site maps dam removals in California, Oregon and Washington; and spatially joins them to endangered and migratory fish habitat.

The dam records come from the American Rivers Dam Removal Database. This dataset lists 333 removals for California, Oregon & Washington. 290 of these removals are on the map. 42 included no location, and were unable to be recovered. One row is a duplicate. The Methods tab explains this further.

There are two types of fish species layers that sit beneath the dams layer:

  • ESA critical habitat: where the federal government has formally designated habitat for a threatened or endangered fish.
  • Presence layers: what the state and federal agencies that survey these fish have actually recorded.

FINDINGS

One interesting takeaway from this whole process was that in some cases, the dams themselves defined the mapped fish habitat. At some of the biggest removals on the map, the fish data stops at the dam or leaves a gap around it, because that was where the fish were unable to travel upstream.

  • On the Klamath, Iron Gate Dam sits 0.24 m outside the watershed range California uses for Chinook salmon. The range ends at the dam because the dam ended the run.
  • Upstream, Copco No. 1, Copco 2 and Jenny Creek have no observed fish data at all. Their only matches are whole-watershed ranges, while Oregon's records resume above them at John C. Boyle. Fish are recorded below and above, and there's a gap in the middle that follows a state line, not the river.
  • Condit Dam on the White Salmon River is the only dam in the project with chum salmon critical habitat, and the state's fish layer maps no chum within a kilometer of it.

None of this is an error in the sources. Each agency mapped what was there, and for decades it was a dam.

THIS PROJECT

This project is not affiliated with any of the agencies whose data it uses. Where it has corrected or questioned a source record, the Methods tab says so and gives the evidence. One layer, Southern Oregon / Northern California Coast coho critical habitat, was rebuilt by this project from the text of the federal regulation (50 CFR 226.210), because the agency never published it as a map. It is drawn in a different shade and marked as derived wherever it appears.

The code for this site is on GitHub at nathanhwyoung/west-coast-dam-removals.
