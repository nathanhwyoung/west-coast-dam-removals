Every dataset below is public and was downloaded as a dated snapshot, so the map can be traced back to the exact files it was built from. None of it is fetched live.

Dam removals

  • American Rivers Dam Removal Database, March 2026 release (figshare version 13, CC BY 4.0). The list of removals, their names, rivers, heights and years, and most coordinates.
  • National Aquatic Barrier Inventory, v4.3.0, compiled by the Southeast Aquatic Resources Partnership (SARP) and the National Fish Habitat Partnership (NFHP), downloaded 2026-09-08. Coordinates for 12 removals American Rivers left blank, and an independent cross-check of the fish results.
  • Corrections to individual records cite their own evidence (agency project pages, news reports, imagery). The evidence travels with each corrected dam.

Endangered Species Act critical habitat

  • NOAA Fisheries (National Marine Fisheries Service, NMFS) West Coast Region, ESA Critical Habitat, edition of 2023-07-17 (the current one; NMFS has issued no update since). Chinook, coho, steelhead, chum, sockeye, green sturgeon, eulachon.
  • U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS), critical habitat shapefiles (national bundle from ECOS, the Environmental Conservation Online System), edition of 2026-08-31. Bull trout, Lost River sucker, shortnose sucker, Warner sucker, Santa Ana sucker, Little Kern golden trout, delta smelt, tidewater goby, Owens tui chub.
  • Southern Oregon / Northern California Coast (SONCC) coho critical habitat: reconstructed by this project, not published by any agency. Built from the regulation's text, 50 CFR 226.210 in the Code of Federal Regulations, using coho distribution from the Oregon and California Departments of Fish and Wildlife (ODFW and CDFW, below), NOAA's range for the SONCC coho Evolutionarily Significant Unit, or ESU (published as ds803, 2025, in CDFW's Biogeographic Information and Observation System, or BIOS), for the ESU's extent, and U.S. Geological Survey (USGS) StreamStats to delineate the basins above the regulation's boundary dams.

Where fish are present

Oregon

  • Oregon Department of Fish and Wildlife (ODFW), Fish Habitat Distribution, edition 1167_5.

Washington

  • Washington Department of Fish and Wildlife (WDFW), Statewide Washington Integrated Fish Distribution (SWIFD), maintained jointly with the Northwest Indian Fisheries Commission (NWIFC), published 2026-04-17, downloaded 2026-09-11.

California, from the CDFW Biogeographic Information and Observation System (BIOS) public data library, downloaded 2026-09-11:

  • ds326, Coho distribution (CDFW, August 2025)
  • ds340, Winter steelhead distribution (CDFW, June 2012)
  • ds341, Summer steelhead distribution (CDFW, October 2009)
  • ds981, California Coastal Chinook distribution (NOAA, 2005)
  • ds982, Central Valley spring-run Chinook distribution (NOAA, 2005)
  • ds1325, Eulachon range (CDFW; generated by PISCES, below)
  • ds2673, Pacific lamprey historical range and current distribution, California (USFWS, 2021)

Across all three states

  • USFWS, Pacific Lamprey Known Observations and Distribution, Washington, Oregon and Idaho (2022), downloaded 2026-09-11.

California species ranges

  • PISCES 2.0.4, Center for Watershed Sciences, University of California, Davis. Cite Santos, N.R., Katz, J.V.E., Moyle, P.B. and Viers, J.H. (2014), Environmental Modelling & Software 53, 13-26. Used under its noncommercial, attribution, share-alike terms.

Checking the results

  • USGS National Hydrography Dataset (NHD), via The National Map, to name the channel at a dam where the fish layers don't.

Base map

  • Shaded relief: elevation from Terrain Tiles on Amazon Web Services (AWS), which package public-domain data from the USGS 3D Elevation Program, the Shuttle Radar Topography Mission (SRTM) and other national surveys.
  • Lakes, reservoirs and region names: Natural Earth, 1:10m, public domain.
  • State outlines and coastline: U.S. Census Bureau cartographic boundary files, 2023, 1:500,000, public domain.

Software

  • D3 v7.9.0 (ISC license), served with the page. The data were prepared with Python, R and GDAL.
  • Claude, Anthropic's AI model, used through Claude Code. The code for the build scripts, the audits and the site was written collaboratively by Claude and the author, and Claude drafted the text in these tabs. The project's scope, its rules and the decisions on individual records were made by the author, who reviewed the results and edited the text.

The data behind the map

Download the dams (dams.geojson, 148 KB): all 290 mapped removals, with the species and critical habitat matched at each one and the grade of every match.

The full join results, the audits and the scripts that built everything are in the GitHub repository, in its data/ and build/ folders. The README describes each file.
